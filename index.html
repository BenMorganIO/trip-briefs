Critical Alpine Parameters

These variables decide whether the route is safe. Check each one before the go/no-go call.

What to Watch

Freezing Level (0C Isotherm):Crucial for glacier travel. High freezing levels weaken snow bridges over crevasses and increase rockfall and wet loose avalanche risk.

Wind at Ridgeline:The Ramparts are highly exposed. Sustained high winds cause balance issues,severe wind chill,and can develop localized wind slabs if fresh snow exists.

Cloud Cover &Visibility:Low clouds or fog over the Asulkan Glacier create disorienting whiteout conditions. Safe crevasse navigation becomes nearly impossible without GPS.

Precipitation Type:Rain on late-season snow or glacial ice makes surfaces incredibly slick. New snow obscures crevasses and creates fresh avalanche problems.

Convective Energy (Thunderstorm Risk):Afternoon convective buildups are common in early fall. Being caught on the exposed ridge during lightning is a fatal hazard.

Important Notes

Always download offline maps for the entire route area beforeyou lose cell signal. Rogers Pass has very limited cell coverage,and the alpine areas have none.

GPX tracks are a reference,not a guarantee. Conditions change,snow obscures the trail,and route-finding skills are essential. Carry a physical map and compass as backup.

The Asulkan Traverse is a mountaineering route,not a hiking trail. A GPX track shows where others went,but glacier conditions,snow coverage,and rock stability vary year to year and even week to week.

Route Hazards

The Asulkan Traverse is an alpine climbing route (D+ 5.4 per the Rogers Pass Alpine Guide by David P. Jones,2012). Each hazard below is sourced to the person who reported it and when.

!

Crevasse Risk —Asulkan Glacier Critical

Stay climber's right on the Asulkan Glacier to avoid heavy crevasses and icefall danger. The Illecillewaet Neve presents late-season blue ice with visible crevasse sags requiring crampons and ice axe.

Source:Mason Stamile,Mountain Project,Mar 5,2025·Chris Gooliaff (ACMG/IFMGA),MCR,Aug 18,2022
!

5.4 Crux —Dome Peak Chimney Critical

A short 5.4 chimney/step on the south ridge of The Dome. Snow step-kicking leads up to the crux,then a chimney you climb and exit. The overall route grade is D+ 5.4. An unroped fall here or on the Ramparts would be fatal.

Source:Anthony Bonello,blog,Jul 31,2009·Chris Gooliaff (ACMG),MCR,Aug 18,2022·Rogers Pass Alpine Guideby David P. Jones (High Col Press,2012)
!

The Ramparts —Knife-Edge Exposure High

Narrow,blocky ridge of pristine Selkirk granite with immense drops on both sides. Described as a "knife edge" and "sidewalk in the sky." Key route-finding rule:stick to the ridge crest —rock quality deteriorates rapidly off the crest. Much of the route is exposed class 2 with thrilling exposure.

Source:Anthony Bonello,blog,Jul 31,2009·Adam Smith,FKT route page,Sep 18,2025
!

Rockfall —Loose Selkirk Rock High

The Selkirk Mountains are notorious for loose,blocky rock. Natural rockfall is a constant threat as permafrost melts late in the season. Climbers can easily dislodge rocks onto partners below. Helmets are mandatory.

Source:Chris Gooliaff (ACMG),MCR,Aug 18,2022·Anthony Bonello,blog,Jul 31,2009
!

Weather Volatility —Rogers Pass High

Rogers Pass weather is notoriously volatile. Sudden rainstorms coat rock in a slick "rain crust." Fog causes whiteout conditions on the glacier. Afternoon thunderstorm risk is a real hazard in early fall.

Source:Adam Smith,FKT trip report,Sep 18,2025(notes afternoon thunderstorm risk and water scarcity by mid-September)
!

Descent to Asulkan Pass —Loose Scree High

The final ~200m descent into Asulkan Pass from the west is very firm scree/gravel/heather/choss requiring full attention. This is not a trail —it is loose terrain that demands careful footing.

Source:Chris Gooliaff (ACMG),MCR,Aug 18,2022
!

Wildlife —Grizzly Bears Moderate

This is prime grizzly bear habitat. Parks Canada frequently issues bear warnings for the Asulkan and Illecillewaet valleys. A "Group of 4 restriction" may be mandated. Carry bear spray,easily accessible.

Source:Parks Canada,Glacier National Park advisories
!

Avalanche Terrain (ATES Class 3) Moderate

Parks Canada rates the Asulkan Valley as Class 3 - Complex. While primarily a winter/spring hazard,an early September snowstorm could deposit enough snow on steep glacier ice or rock slabs to cause a slab avalanche.

Source:Parks Canada,Glacier National Park·Rogers Pass Alpine Guideby David P. Jones (2012)

Go / No-Go Checklist

Before committing,verify ALL of the following:

1. Freezing level is below 2,500m (snow bridges intact)

2. Ridgeline winds under 30 km/h sustained

3. No thunderstorm risk in the forecast window

4. Visibility is clear (no fog/whiteout risk)

5. No fresh snow on the route in the past 48 hours

6. Group has glacier travel and crevasse rescue skills

7. All members have helmets,harnesses,and required gear

8. Bear warning status checked with Parks Canada

9. Filed a trip plan with Parks Canada registration

10. Mountain Conditions Report reviewed

Route Overview

The Asulkan Traverse is a high-alpine horseshoe circuit starting and ending at Illecillewaet Campground in Glacier National Park.

Key Facts

Location:Glacier National Park,Rogers Pass,British Columbia,Canada

Start/End:Illecillewaet Campground (51.2661N,-117.4952W)

Distance:24.5 - 27 km (depending on exact route)

Elevation Gain:2,300 - 2,600 m

Difficulty:D+ 5.4 alpine (per Rogers Pass Alpine Guide,David P. Jones,2012)

Typical Duration:12-14 hours single push,or 2-3 days with hut stops

Rating:ATES Class 3 - Complex avalanche terrain

Key Waypoints

The route progresses clockwise from the campground through forest,alpine ridge,glacier,and back to valley.

  1. Illecillewaet Campground (Start/End)
    Trailhead at 51.2661N,-117.4952W. Parking and Parks Canada registration.
  2. Abbott Ridge &Mount Afton
    Grueling initial ascent up the Abbott Ridge Trail to gain the ridge. Forest transitions to alpine. ~1,200m of hiking (Hueniken,2012).
  3. The Ramparts ("Sidewalk in the Sky")
    Narrow,blocky ridge of pristine Selkirk granite. "Knife edge" exposure (Bonello,2009). Stick to the crest —rock quality deteriorates off it. Exposed class 2 (Smith,2025).
  4. Dome Peak (Technical Crux)
    Short 5.4 chimney on the south ridge of The Dome (Bonello,2009;Gooliaff,2022). Snow step-kicking leads to the chimney. Most exposed section.
  5. Sapphire Col &Sapphire Col Hut
    Rudimentary metal bivouac shelter at the col. Emergency use or multi-day pacing. Heavily crevassed approach.
  6. Jupiter Complex (Castor,Pollux,Leda Peaks)
    Subsequent peaks on the backside of the horseshoe. 4th class with easy 5th steps (Stamile,MP,2025). More exposed ridge travel.
  7. Asulkan Pass
    High pass transitioning from ridge to glacier descent. Final ~200m descent is loose scree/gravel/heather/choss (Gooliaff,2022). Route-finding critical.
  8. Asulkan Glacier Descent
    Glacier travel down to the hut. Stay climber's right to avoid heavy crevasses and icefall (Stamile,2025). Rope up required.
  9. Asulkan Cabin (ACC Hut)
    Fully equipped Alpine Club of Canada cabin at 51.2143N,-117.4695W. Reservations required for overnight.
  10. Asulkan Valley Trail (Return)
    Trail descent from the hut back to Illecillewaet Campground. Well-marked but long. Creeks may be dry by mid-September (Smith,2025).
