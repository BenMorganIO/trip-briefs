Critical Alpine Parameters

These variables decide whether the route is safe. Check each one before the go/no-go call.

What to Watch

Freezing Level (0C Isotherm): Crucial for glacier travel. High freezing levels weaken snow bridges over crevasses and increase rockfall and wet loose avalanche risk.

Wind at Ridgeline: The Ramparts are highly exposed. Sustained high winds cause balance issues, severe wind chill, and can develop localized wind slabs if fresh snow exists.

Cloud Cover & Visibility: Low clouds or fog over the Asulkan Glacier create disorienting whiteout conditions. Safe crevasse navigation becomes nearly impossible without GPS.

Precipitation Type: Rain on late-season snow or glacial ice makes surfaces incredibly slick. New snow obscures crevasses and creates fresh avalanche problems.

Convective Energy (Thunderstorm Risk): Afternoon convective buildups are common in early fall. Being caught on the exposed ridge during lightning is a fatal hazard.

Route Hazards

The Asulkan Traverse is an alpine climbing route (PD+ to AD, 5.4 rock) requiring glacier travel skills. These are the hazards to assess before and during your trip.

!

Crevasse Risk on Asulkan Glacier Critical

By early September, the firn line is high and much of the lower glacier is bare hard ice. Upper sections near Sapphire Col have numerous wide, open crevasses. Snow bridges are at their thinnest and weakest point of the year, or entirely melted out. Rope up, carry crevasse rescue gear, and be proficient in self-rescue.

!

Exposure & Technical Climbing Critical

The ridge requires climbing moves up to 5.4 with significant exposure. 'A+ ridge running' with several 'spicy moves' on steep rock. An unroped fall on the Ramparts or Dome Peak sections would be fatal. The 'Sidewalk in the Sky' has immense drops on both sides.

!

Rockfall High

The Selkirk Mountains are notorious for loose, blocky rock. Natural rockfall is a constant threat as permafrost melts late in the season. Climbers can easily dislodge rocks onto partners below. Steep, sun-exposed slopes are particularly prone to releasing rocks as the day warms up. Helmets are mandatory.

!

Weather Volatility High

Rogers Pass weather is notoriously volatile. Sudden rainstorms can coat rock in a slick 'rain crust' making scrambling exceptionally dangerous. Heavy cloud cover and fog cause whiteout conditions on the Asulkan Glacier, making route-finding through crevasse fields nearly impossible without GPS or compass.

!

Early September Transition High

Transitional season: you may face continued heat melting weak snow bridges and loosening rock, OR the first winter storms. Early snowstorms deposit a thin, slippery layer over rock (making 5.4 climbing extremely hazardous) and hide open crevasses without providing a structurally sound snow bridge. Check the Mountain Conditions Report before departure.

!

Wildlife - Grizzly Bears Moderate

This is prime grizzly bear habitat. Parks Canada frequently issues bear warnings for the Asulkan and Illecillewaet valleys. A 'Group of 4 restriction' may be mandated by park authorities. Carry bear spray, easily accessible on your person.

!

Avalanche Terrain (ATES Class 3) Moderate

Parks Canada rates the Asulkan Valley as Class 3 - Complex. While primarily a winter/spring hazard, an early September snowstorm could deposit enough snow on steep glacier ice or rock slabs to cause a small but highly consequential slab avalanche.

Go / No-Go Checklist

Before committing, verify ALL of the following:

1. Freezing level is below 2,500m (snow bridges intact)

2. Ridgeline winds under 30 km/h sustained

3. No thunderstorm risk in the forecast window

4. Visibility is clear (no fog/whiteout risk)

6. Group has glacier travel and crevasse rescue skills

Route Overview

The Asulkan Traverse is a high-alpine horseshoe circuit starting and ending at Illecillewaet Campground in Glacier National Park.

Key Facts

Location: Glacier National Park, Rogers Pass, British Columbia, Canada

Start/End: Illecillewaet Campground (51.2661N, -117.4952W)

Distance: 24.5 - 27 km (depending on exact route)

Elevation Gain: 2,300 - 2,600 m

Difficulty: PD+ to AD alpine, 5.4 rock climbing

Typical Duration: 12-14 hours single push, or 2-3 days with hut stops

Rating: ATES Class 3 - Complex avalanche terrain

Key Waypoints

The route progresses clockwise from the campground through forest, alpine ridge, glacier, and back to valley.

  1. Illecillewaet Campground (Start/End)
    Trailhead at 51.2661N, -117.4952W. Parking and Parks Canada registration.
  2. Abbott Ridge & Mount Afton
    Grueling initial ascent up the Abbott Ridge Trail to gain the ridge. Forest transitions to alpine.
  3. The Ramparts ('Sidewalk in the Sky')
    Narrow, blocky ridge of pristine Selkirk granite with immense drops on both sides. Highly exposed 3rd-4th class scrambling.
  4. Dome Peak (Technical Crux)
    The crux of the route. 5.4 rock climbing moves required to ascend/descend. Most exposed section.
  5. Sapphire Col & Sapphire Col Hut
    Rudimentary metal bivouac shelter at the col. Emergency use or multi-day pacing. Heavily crevassed approach.
  6. Jupiter Complex (Castor, Pollux, Leda Peaks)
    Subsequent peaks on the backside of the horseshoe. More exposed ridge travel.
  7. Asulkan Pass
    High pass transitioning from ridge to glacier descent. Route-finding critical here.
  8. Asulkan Glacier Descent
    Glacier travel down to the hut. Crevasse risk is high. Rope up required. Late-season bergschrund may be open.
  9. Asulkan Cabin (ACC Hut)
    Fully equipped Alpine Club of Canada cabin at 51.2143N, -117.4695W. World-class valley views. Reservations required for overnight.
  10. Asulkan Valley Trail (Return)
    Trail descent from the hut back to Illecillewaet Campground. Well-marked but long.
