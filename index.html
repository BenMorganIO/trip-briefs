Hazard assessment

This is a mountaineering route, not a hiking trail. Conditions and required equipment vary.

CRITICAL

Crevasses and snow bridges

Asulkan Glacier can be heavily crevassed. Late-season bridges may be thin or gone. Rope up and use a trained glacier team with rescue equipment.

CRITICAL

Exposure and climbing

The Ramparts and Dome Peak include exposed scrambling and climbing up to 5.4. Falls can be fatal. Use appropriate protection and retreat before conditions deteriorate.

HIGH

Rockfall and loose rock

Loose Selkirk rock and warming conditions can release blocks. Wear helmets, manage spacing, and avoid moving below other climbers.

HIGH

Weather and visibility

Rain, fog, whiteout, wind, lightning, and freezing temperatures can arrive quickly. Wet rock and obscured glacier features are serious hazards.

HIGH

Early September transition

Expect either continued melt and weakening bridges or first snow and ice. Fresh snow can conceal crevasses and make the ridge slick.

MODERATE

Wildlife and regulations

Grizzly habitat surrounds the valleys. Check current Parks Canada warnings and group-size restrictions. Carry bear spray accessibly.

Minimum equipment discussion

Helmet, harness, rope, crampons, mountaineering axe, crevasse rescue equipment, navigation backups, emergency shelter, headlamp, first aid, communication device, and bear spray. A light rock rack may be required depending on the team.

Go/no-go checks

Confirm glacier conditions, weather, freezing level, ridge wind, visibility, recent snow, Parks Canada advisories, hut or camping arrangements, team glacier skills, and a filed trip plan. If any critical item is uncertain, choose a lower-consequence objective.
