Route Overview

The Fitzwilliam Trail approaches a high alpine basin below Mount Fitzwilliam (2,902 m), Bucephalus Peak (2,763 m), Frontier Peak (2,682 m), and Kataka Mountain (2,679 m). The route is a 3-day backpacking trip with scrambling side quests from an alpine camp.

Key Facts

Trailhead: Yellowhead Lake boat launch parking, 54 km east of Mount Robson Visitor Centre on Hwy 16. Self-registration kiosk is ~1.3 km along the pipeline right-of-way on the south side of the highway. Coordinates approximately 52.8608N, 118.5347W.

Rockingham Creek Camp: 7.2 km from trailhead, ~600 m gain. Six tent pads, pit toilet, bear pole. Water from creek.

Alpine Wilderness Camp: ~13.5 km from trailhead, ~800-1,000 m gain, located at the north end of the first alpine lake in Fitzwilliam Basin. Three tent pads, bear pole, open pit toilet. No fires.

Peaks: Mount Fitzwilliam 2,902 m; Bucephalus Peak 2,763 m; Frontier Peak 2,682 m; Kataka Mountain 2,679 m. The ridge connecting Fitzwilliam, Bucephalus, and Frontier continues south to Clairvaux and then north to Kataka.

Sources: PeakBliss (Aug 2023); ClubTread (Sep 2010); Hiking Prince George; Mums Who Hike; BC Parks.

Itinerary

  1. Day 1 — Approach to Fitzwilliam Basin~13.5 km, ~900 m gain. Hike from Yellowhead Lake to Rockingham Creek Camp (7.2 km), then continue past the headwall to the alpine wilderness camp at the north end of the first alpine lake. Set up camp and scout the basin.
  2. Day 2 — Side QuestsScramble Bucephalus Peak from the Fitzwilliam/Bucephalus col (~45 min from col, more stable rock than Fitzwilliam). Traverse to the Bucephalus/Frontier col and ascend Frontier Peak via its NW Ridge (Class 2 scrambling over boulder field). Alternatively, descend from Bucephalus and cross the basin to attempt Kataka Mountain (2,679 m) on the east side. Route beta for Kataka is limited; treat as exploratory.
  3. Day 3 — Exit~13.5 km out, ~900 m descent. Pack up and return to the trailhead via Rockingham Creek Camp.

PeakBliss — Aug 2023

Modern route report · peakbliss.ca

Completed Fitzwilliam and Bucephalus as a 14-hour day hike: 24 km, 2,000 m gain. Describes Fitzwilliam rock as loose and rotten, requiring a helmet. Bucephalus is more stable rock, about 45 minutes from the col. Gore-Tex boots and gaiters recommended for wet swamp and mud on the approach. The ridge connecting Fitzwilliam and Bucephalus continues south to Frontier and Clairvaux.

ClubTread / 4peat — Sep 16-18, 2010

Backpacking trip report · clubtread.com

Spent three days in the basin. Trail is 13 km, almost 1,000 m gain. Rockingham Creek Camp has six tent pads and a bear pole. The alpine wilderness camp (north end of first alpine lake) has three tent pads, a bear pole, and an open pit toilet. The headwall has a hidden trail below Kataka Mountain that bypasses the main scree slope. Mumm Basin is not recommended as an exit. The ridge has various named peaks: Fitzwilliam, Bucephalus, Frontier, Clairvaux, Kataka, and Miette Hill.

Route Hazards

Fitzwilliam Basin is a wilderness backpacking and scrambling route. Conditions must be assessed independently before committing.

!

Loose rock on Mount Fitzwilliam High

Fitzwilliam upper mountain is described as loose and rotten. Rockfall is a serious hazard. Helmets are strongly advised for any scrambling on Fitzwilliam.

Source: PeakBliss (Aug 2023)
!

Wet swamp and overgrown trail Moderate

The approach trail has muddy sections, swamp, and bushwhack. Gore-Tex boots and gaiters are recommended. The trail can be overgrown and faint in places.

Sources: PeakBliss; Mums Who Hike
!

Bear country and poor camp facilities High

Backcountry bear habitat. The bear pole at Rockingham Creek may have missing rope (2010 report). Alpine camp has no bear hang, only a pole. No fires permitted. Proper food storage and bear awareness are essential.

Sources: ClubTread (Sep 2010); Hiking Prince George
!

Limited beta for Kataka Mountain Moderate

Detailed route descriptions for Kataka Mountain are scarce. It is on the east side of the basin. Treat as an exploratory scramble with no known route.

Source: ClubTread (Sep 2010); Stevensong
!

Weather exposure at elevation High

Alpine camp is at ~2,100 m. Peaks exceed 2,600 m. Early September can bring snow, cold, and high wind. Be prepared for sudden weather changes.

Source: General alpine knowledge
