Route Overview

Mumm Peak (2,964 m) rises north of Berg Lake in Mount Robson Provincial Park, on the Alberta–BC boundary. Named after British climber Arnold L. Mumm (1859–1927); the name dates to 1911 when A.O. Wheeler found a cairn placed in 1910 by Collie, Mumm, and guide Moritz Inderbinen. The standard route is a difficult scramble from Berg Lake Campground via the (officially closed) Mumm Basin trail. The ascent follows the south rib; the descent loops back via the eastern Mumm Basin Trail through Robson Pass.

Key Waypoints

The route follows the Berg Lake Trail to Toboggan Falls, then the closed Mumm Basin trail over a rockfall debris field, up the south rib to a loose gully, through three summit cliff bands, and a short extremely exposed traverse to the true summit. Descent loops back via the eastern Mumm Basin Trail through Robson Pass. No published waypoint coordinates exist in text; the route sequence is from Spectacular Mountains (Jul 27, 2025).

  1. Berg Lake Campground (Start)
    Base camp at Berg Lake. Requires backcountry reservation via BC Parks. Approach via Berg Lake Trail (~22 km from trailhead). GPX track start point.
  2. Toboggan Falls
    Turn right onto the Mumm Basin trail at Toboggan Falls, same approach as for Mount Anne-Alice.
  3. Mumm Basin Trail (Closed Section)
    Trail is officially closed due to rockfall that obliterated the path. A sign warns "Proceed at your own risk." Navigate across the debris field and pick up the trail on the far side.
  4. Rockfall Debris Field
    Unstable angular blocks with deep gaps and cracks. Careful footing required. Pick up trail again on the far side.
  5. Alpine Lake (Mumm Basin)
    Small alpine lake in Mumm Basin. Short off-trail detour. Vibrant turquoise hues surrounded by vertical cliffs. Transition from trail to open alpine terrain.
  6. South Rib
    Ascend the rib running south from the summit mass. Lots of rubble, loose choss, relentless grade. Views of glaciers, lakes, and Mount Robson improve with elevation.
  7. Loose Gully / Lower Cliff Band
    Horribly loose weakness and rubbly gully. Helmet zone — significant rockfall danger with two or more climbers. Breach the lower cliff band.
  8. Summit Ridge Cliff Bands (Three)
    Three cliff bands on the summit ridge. The final band involves a few 5.4 moves and a short (3–4 m) extremely exposed traverse on the north side of the ridge. Note: Vern's trip report warns of a difficult chimney with overhanging chockstone on the west side; Ben and Keegan explored the east side instead, which worked but was still difficult.
  9. Summit (2,964 m)
    Cairn with old registers, including a Japanese climber's pebble noting 2007 and 2009 ascents. Spectacular views of the Mural Glacier and Mount Robson massif.
  10. Descent: Eastern Mumm Basin Trail
    Downclimb all difficult sections, then take the eastern part of the Mumm Basin Trail down to Robson Pass Campground. Use snow patches to speed up descent. Rejoin the main Berg Lake Trail back to camp.

What to Watch

Precipitation & Rock Conditions: The final 3–4 m north-side traverse and the 5.4 summit moves are extremely exposed. Wet or snowy rock makes the true summit a no-go. Check Mountain-Forecast and Environment Canada for precipitation and freezing level.

Freezing Level (0C Isotherm): High freezing levels weaken snow bridges on the year-round snow slope near the summit and increase rockfall risk on the debris field and loose gully.

Wind at Summit Ridge: The summit ridge is highly exposed. Sustained high winds make the 5.4 moves and the north-side traverse extremely dangerous.

Cloud Cover & Visibility: Fog or low clouds on the summit ridge create disorienting conditions. Route-finding through the three cliff bands becomes critical without visibility.

Berg Lake Trail Status: The approach requires the Berg Lake Trail to be open. Check BC Parks advisories for closures and washouts before committing.

Route Hazards

Mumm Peak is a difficult scramble (Difficult/Kane, T6/SAC) with a few 5.4 moves near the true summit. Each hazard below is sourced to the person who reported it and when.

!

Rockfall on Closed Mumm Basin Trail High

The Mumm Basin trail is officially closed where rockfall has obliterated the path. A sign warns "Proceed at your own risk." The debris field consists of unstable angular blocks with deep gaps and cracks.

Source: Ben Morgan, Spectacular Mountains, Jul 27, 2025
!

Loose Rock in Summit Gully High

The route breaches the lower cliff band through a "horribly loose weakness" and "rubbly gully." Significant rockfall danger with two or more climbers. Helmets strongly advised — the author explicitly regretted not having one.

Source: Ben Morgan, Spectacular Mountains, Jul 27, 2025
!

Extreme Exposure on Final Ridge Critical

A short (3–4 m) extremely exposed traverse on the north side of the ridge near the top. The true summit involves "a few 5.4 moves" and should only be attempted if comfortable with difficult scrambling. An unroped fall here would be fatal. Vern's trip report warns of a difficult chimney with overhanging chockstone on the west side; the east-side variation also involves 5.4 moves.

Source: Ben Morgan, Spectacular Mountains, Jul 27, 2025 · Vern, Google Drive trip report (undated)
!

Wet or Snowy Rock — Go/No-Go High

The final traverse and 5.4 summit moves are "definitely not a good idea if the rock is wet or snowy." Only attempt the true summit in favourable weather conditions with dry rock.

Source: Ben Morgan, Spectacular Mountains, Jul 27, 2025
!

Year-Round Snow Slope Near Summit Moderate

A ~100 m snow slope near the summit is present year-round. An ice axe is recommended, and a rope "would aid in the going down" for the descent. Crampons may be needed if the snow is icy.

Source: SummitPost community page, Mumm Peak
!

Class 4 Crux Near Summit Moderate

"A difficult scramble. Short section of class 4 near the top, but mostly class 2 and 3." The class 4 section is the technical crux of the route.

Source: MtnNinja, ClubTread, Sep 10, 2010
!

Berg Lake Trail Closures & Washouts Moderate

The Berg Lake Trail has had repeated closures and washouts. The approach to Mumm Peak depends entirely on this trail being open. Verify current status via BC Parks advisories before committing.

Source: BC Parks, Berg Lake Trail advisories

Go / No-Go Checklist

Before committing, verify ALL of the following:

1. Rock is dry on the summit ridge and final traverse (no wet or snowy rock)

2. No precipitation in the forecast window

3. Summit ridge winds under 30 km/h sustained

4. Visibility is clear (no fog/whiteout risk for route-finding through cliff bands)

5. Berg Lake Trail is open (check BC Parks advisories)

6. Backcountry camping reservation confirmed at Berg Lake

7. All party members have helmets

8. Ice axe and rope carried for the snow slope and exposed traverse

9. Party is comfortable with class 4 / 5.4 scrambling with extreme exposure

10. Voluntary Self-Registration filed at Berg Lake trailhead or Robson Visitor Centre
